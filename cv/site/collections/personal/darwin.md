Charles Robert Darwin was an English naturalist, geologist, and biologist,
widely known for his contributions to evolutionary biology. His proposition
that all species of life have descended from a common ancestor is now widely
accepted and considered a foundational concept in science.

Darwin published his theory of evolution with compelling evidence in his 1859
book *On the Origin of Species*, overcoming scientific rejection of earlier
concepts of transmutation of species. After the Beagle voyage (1831–1836),
he lived a largely secluded life at Down House in Kent, where he continued
his research — including a remarkable late-career body of work on plant
physiology and behaviour — until his death in 1882.
