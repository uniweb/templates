# Designing with Constraints

A blank canvas is paralyzing. A canvas with edges is an invitation. The limits you're given — or the ones you impose — are what turn a vague idea into a specific one.

## Limits force decisions

When everything is possible, nothing is decided. A tight palette, a fixed grid, a word count: each one removes a thousand bad options so you can see the good ones.

## Useful constraints to adopt

- **One job per screen.** If a page is trying to do three things, it does none of them well.
- **A small type scale.** Two or three sizes go further than ten.
- **A real content budget.** Design with the longest title and the emptiest state, not the demo data.

## Borrow before you build

Most problems you'll face have been solved well by someone. Start from a known-good default and only diverge where you have a reason.

> Creativity loves a box. Give it one.

The point of constraints isn't to make things smaller — it's to make the important things obvious.
