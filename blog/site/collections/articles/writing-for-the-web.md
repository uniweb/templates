# Writing for the Web

Good web writing respects the reader's time. They arrived with a question; your job is to answer it before they give up looking.

## Front-load the point

Say the most important thing first. The inverted pyramid isn't just for journalists — it's how impatient readers actually consume a page.

## Make it scannable

- Short paragraphs beat long ones
- Headings act as signposts
- Lists carry steps and options better than prose

## Cut until it hurts, then stop

Most first drafts are twice as long as they need to be. Read it back and delete every word that isn't doing work.

> The reader is busy. Earn each sentence.

Clarity is a feature. When the writing disappears and only the meaning is left, you've done the job.
